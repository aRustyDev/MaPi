# Local team-ownership fencing proof

This is a synthetic, test-only coordination protocol. It is not connected to Nextgen's grant, team, audit, registry, recovery or ingress code. Its fake RPC endpoints do not authenticate callers. It does not authorize or enable team deactivation in production.

## Protocol and invariants

The proof uses an SQLite Durable Object per project, a project-route registry, and a durable coordinator per team. The coordinator is the authoritative index of the team's owning grants. A grant create first reserves a unique operation, target project, route generation and grant ID at the team coordinator. The target project verifies that reservation and commits its local grant and audit in one transaction. The coordinator reconciles only a matching committed target record after checking the registry route before and after the read. A crash after either commit leaves a durable, idempotently retryable intent; a pending reservation blocks team deletion.

Deactivation moves the coordinator from active to draining, rejecting new reservations. It waits for all reserved operations to reconcile. If any grant remains active it returns an ownership conflict and reopens reservations; otherwise it records a deactivation permit. The home project checks the permit and its local project-generation fence, then commits its team mutation and audit together. Only after reading the authoritative home result does the coordinator mark the team deactivated. A crash between commits leaves the barrier in place and is resumed by retry. There is no atomic transaction across the three objects.

A coordinator cannot release a failed or abandoned grant merely because a lease expired or the target has no row: a delayed writer might still commit. Instead, the target project atomically writes an operation tombstone, or reports that the grant already committed. The target checks that tombstone inside the same transaction as grant creation. The coordinator reconciles that terminal state before lifting its barrier. A grant revoke stays conservatively active at the coordinator until the target's durable revocation is reconciled. Operation IDs and grant IDs are not reused, and duplicate retry does not duplicate audit records.

## Local evidence

Run `node scripts/test-team-ownership-proof.mjs` from MaPi. It starts local workerd with separate registry, team-coordinator, home-project and target-project Durable Objects. The test races reservation against deactivation, explicitly exercises deletion winning first, and separately races target grant creation against its cancellation tombstone. It evicts and reacquires objects after grant creation, revocation and team mutation, retries reconciliation concurrently, injects grant, revoke and team audit failures, tests replay, and verifies a frozen old project route cannot write or provide evidence after the registry generation changes. An unresolved old-generation intent stays pending: the proof does not silently release it after relocation. Expected rejected calls and evictions appear in Wrangler diagnostics only if the runner fails.

## Production work still required

- All project-grant creation, transfer, revocation, deletion, import, administrative and background paths must use the coordinator, including writers in every project object. The proof has no integration with upstream `HasActiveOwningTeamGrant`, Nextgen grant statements or the real team cascade. A side path that writes a grant without a reservation invalidates the safety argument.
- Bootstrap and migration need a complete, consistent ownership index. Fence all relevant existing writers, scan and reconcile every project and existing grant, and establish the authoritative coordinator before allowing deletion or new grants. A partially built index must fail closed.
- RPC callers and all operation, team, project and route identities require authenticated service bindings and authorization. The proof's exposed fake methods trust inputs and provide no production access control. A production coordinator must verify grant/target identity, guard operation reuse and obtain evidence from the current authoritative owner, not accept client-supplied proof.
- Relocation must freeze old owners, copy and reconcile pending intents and tombstones, and switch a strongly consistent registry with fences before activating the new owner. The test deliberately leaves a stale intent blocked; it does not implement its cross-generation transfer or recovery. Independent registry and project restores can roll back committed data or coordinator state; fresh non-reused recovery eras, fencing, reconciliation and explicit session/handoff policy are required before reopening. No PITR or real relocation is tested here.
- The proof cannot atomically read the registry, target project and coordinator. Its double-read route check depends on the relocation protocol correctly fencing writers and preserving committed records. Timeouts, failures, duplicate deliveries, partial restore, unbounded stale intents, coordinator migration and adversarial control-plane events need systematic testing and operational handling. Pending intents can block deletion indefinitely; safe reconciliation takes priority over availability.
- The real team transaction also touches owned users, memberships, authz edges and mutation audit. Their authority and any cross-project effects must be modeled and committed or reconciled with equivalent guarantees. The synthetic audit table is not Nextgen's audit system. No hosted performance, size, isolation, conformance or production security review has been performed.
