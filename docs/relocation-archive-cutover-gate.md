# Relocation cutover: new commits blocked pending archive authority

Status: **real cutover is not authorized or safe to perform on the current code**. The direct registry RPC now rejects every new cutover until an independent archive authority is implemented. Already-committed moves can still resume forward recovery. This is a local code change, not a hosted test or an independent recovery proof. It does not change any existing Woven deployment or establish that one is protected.

`RelocationCoordinator.advance` in `worker/relocation-coordinator.js` proceeds from completed `verifyRelocationHistory` and `reconcileRelocation` to `ProjectRegistry.commitRelocation`, which now rejects new cutovers unconditionally. Its private RPC is gated by `MAPI_RELOCATION_ENABLED` and `MAPI_RELOCATION_CONTROL_TOKEN`; source fencing additionally requires the operator assertion `MAPI_FENCE_WRITERS_DRAINED`. These settings are not proof of an archive or independent custody. Starting a move can still freeze a source and interrupt project traffic; do not enable these controls on the real singleton.

`ProjectRegistry.commitRelocation` in `worker/registry.js` takes only the control secret, project ID and move ID. After checking the current reconciliation checkpoint, it denies a new cutover instead of invoking `commitRegistryMove`. The low-level helper in `worker/registry-move.js` checks the current route and move generation, owner states, identities, matching SHA-256 transfer digest and `completedHistoryCheckpoint`, but has no archive authority and is not itself a production RPC. The retained source chunk chain and matching destination are transfer evidence; they do not themselves establish an independently recoverable encrypted archive.

The synthetic archive and key-provider modules are currently unmounted prototypes. Their local checks and the provisioned synthetic AWS KMS resources do not establish independently administered recovery: the synthetic writer and recovery roles share an administrator identity. The user explicitly chose to keep that gate open. The source review does not imply that a move actually occurred, that this control is exposed publicly, or that any existing Woven deployment has enabled it.

Before a real cutover, a separately authenticated, independently durable authority must bind its verified result to the exact frozen source and move: project and origin, source object and generation, move ID, schema and ownership manifest, ordered table set, chunk count and final digest, archive inventory/seal, immutable key version and applicable retention. The proof must establish that the encrypted archive and key can be recovered under the approved independent-custody policy and that a fresh inaccessible target reconciles before activation. The registry must consume a trustworthy current proof at its cutover boundary, with replay, stale-generation, interruption and concurrent-mover behavior tested. A value or flag supplied by the move writer, a local authority double, or a self-consistent archive alone is not that proof. The exact integration mechanism remains unimplemented; cross-object atomicity, nonrollback, writer drain and failure-domain assumptions must be explicit.

## Proposed enforcement boundary

The user chose a fail-closed archive gate. The implementation must guard the public Durable Object RPC `ProjectRegistry.commitRelocation` itself: a check only in `RelocationCoordinator.advance` can be bypassed by a direct authenticated registry call. A new move in phase `moving` must not reach `commitRegistryMove` without accepted archive evidence. Missing configuration, an unavailable or unverifiable authority, a mismatched identity or digest, expired or unprovable retention, and a changed route, checkpoint or owner attestation must deny the new cutover while keeping the source fenced and the destination staged. Neither `MAPI_RELOCATION_ENABLED`, `MAPI_FENCE_WRITERS_DRAINED`, a synthetic flag nor the control token is archive evidence.

The existing early branch for the *same already-committed move* must remain available for idempotent recovery: the coordinator must be able to finish destination activation and source retirement after a lost acknowledgement. This exception must not change the route again, permit a different move, or create a new generation. A failed or uncertain external check before commit must be resolved by rereading authoritative state; it must not assume a remote action or local route update rolled back.

An eventual independently administered archive service needs a private, authenticated interface that consumes registry-resolved move identity and source attestations, rather than accepting project or digest claims from an HTTP client. Its response must be verifiable against authority configuration outside the archive writer's control and must bind every field listed above. The actual source manifest currently contains schema, sequence and table data; a canonical, complete, bounded manifest commitment and authoritative chunk count are not yet wired into this interface. The current synthetic gateway pins configuration and heads but does not prove a signer is independent or a terminal head reflects a full verification; it is not this cutover authority.

External service calls cannot run inside the registry's synchronous SQLite transaction. A proof fetched before the route CAS is safe only with an independently enforced, nonrollback and nonrevocable retention guarantee covering the cutover and the required recovery period. The registry must re-read route, move, reconciliation and owner evidence after each asynchronous boundary and check the bound archive proof immediately before the transactional CAS; even then, those local checks do not themselves make a remote authority atomic or prevent ABA. If the proof can expire, be revoked or be rolled back between validation and commit, a separately enforced linearization or durable irrevocable grant is necessary. A Worker clock or an observed latency percentile is not a commit-time authority.

The existing AWS synthetic key is symmetric `ENCRYPT_DECRYPT` and cannot sign a proof. Its writer and recovery roles are controlled by the same administrator, so neither the key nor those roles establish the independent verifier this gate requires. No new signing identity, service or custody design is assumed or provisioned by this note. Synthetic rehearsal requires a real, isolated authority with the same enforcement property; a rehearsal switch that skips proof would leave a direct cutover bypass and is not an equivalent gate.

Before an implementation can be accepted, test the actual direct registry RPC and coordinator with missing, forged, wrong-project, wrong-generation, wrong-manifest, stale, incomplete, revoked and unavailable authority evidence; verify no route or move commit, preserve the source fence and staged destination, then prove a valid independent authorization permits exactly one cutover. Exercise lost acknowledgements before and after CAS, simultaneous movers, authority failures and committed-move forward repair. The previously interrupted registry/history integration action and tests remain unverified and must not be treated as passing evidence for this future work.

The fail-closed denial is covered locally by `node scripts/test-registry-move.mjs`: a synthetic move with completed history, matching attestations and reconciliation remains uncommitted under concurrent direct calls; an already-committed move can be retried. The test does not demonstrate an accepted archive proof, independent custody, or a restore. The existing end-to-end relocation rehearsal expects successful cutover and must be updated when a real authority is integrated; it cannot currently establish cutover evidence.

The synthetic staging policy is a separate seven-day minimum and is not a real-project retention policy. No rehearsal bypass is authorized. An independently administered proof and its positive authorization path are not implemented. Preserve the retired-source fence and replay data until the applicable verified archive and retention policy permit cleanup. A real cutover also requires fresh read-only inventory and backup verification, all remaining staging gates, and the user's separate explicit cutover authorization.
