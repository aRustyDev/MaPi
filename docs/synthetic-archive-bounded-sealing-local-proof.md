# Synthetic bounded sealing: local prototype

`worker/synthetic-relocation-archive.js` has an unmounted, synthetic-only bounded sealing path. It has not been deployed, connected to a real project or cutover, or tested with the provisioned live KMS key after these changes. The previously recorded live KMS/local-R2 run predates this implementation. It does not establish independent custody, a protected hosted archive, or production recovery.

`beginSyntheticSealing` writes an immutable authority-required mode marker and starts a sealing job. `advanceSyntheticSealing` authenticates at most eight source chunks or one selector-list page per call. Its encrypted checkpoint and authority head bind the independently supplied source commitment, archive and key-envelope identity, source chain, ordered inventory, revision and predecessor head. After it has checked all chunk and part selectors and the exact terminal source digest, it commits a terminal authority head binding the exact expected legacy-format seal digest **before** making that seal visible. A subsequent call publishes or reads back that exact seal; the final exact selector check rejects extra seal-selector entries. An uncertain authority acknowledgement requires readback from the independent authority; an uncertain publication is retried with identical seal content and the normal nonce-reservation protocol. A terminal head authorizes publication but alone does not prove that publication succeeded.

The mode marker makes current legacy archive-write, one-call seal/verify, bounded verify and restore entry points reject the protected archive unless the protected mode and authority arguments are supplied. The opt-in `verifySyntheticAuthorizedArchive` checks the pinned terminal sealing head and seal before and after a full-history scan; it remains a **one-call, unbounded** verifier. The bounded verifier also accepts a sealing authority argument and binds the exact sealing-head digest in its checkpoint and head. Verified restore accepts the same sealing authority, rechecks both authority heads, and binds the verification-head digest in its staged destination checkpoint. Bounded verification processes up to eight chunks or one listing page per call; protected restore applies up to eight chunks per call. The existing single-scan interfaces remain available for unmarked synthetic archives and do not acquire authority by being present.

The mode marker is not an independent fence: before creating it, an operator must verify that all legacy Worker versions, writers, readers, calls and callbacks are drained and that archive business writers are closed. An old in-flight call can pass its earlier marker check before the marker exists and later commit a write or destination callback. Current code rechecks around those boundaries and fails when it observes the marker, but cannot revoke a write or target callback already dispatched. A target can even commit its local `complete` flag before a post-call head check fails; the target must remain inaccessible, that flag is not activation authority, and independent reconciliation is required. All seal writers, including legacy code, must be excluded by the external closure.

Safety also requires an independently authenticated, linearizable and nonrollback authority that enforces one sealing job per archive, exact predecessor and increasing revisions, and terminal immutable sealing and verification heads; a separately protected pin; and an archive namespace that cannot be overwritten, deleted or rolled back throughout the data key lifetime, verification and recovery. Mode-marker deletion/rollback permits legacy code to read the seal; the local test demonstrates this as a failure, not as an acceptable retention property. If the marker disappears while a seal publication is in flight, the post-check can report the observed loss but cannot retract the already published seal or prevent a legacy reader from accepting it during the gap. A remove-and-restore between checks can evade observation entirely. Namespace write closure and nonrollback storage are therefore mandatory, not established by marker rechecks. The local authority and filesystem store are mutable test doubles, not evidence those controls exist.

All newly created synthetic verification heads now have **version 2** and expose a terminal bit authenticated against the encrypted checkpoint phase. Prior version-1 prototype verification heads are rejected and require a fresh synthetic verification job. Sealing and the seal's legacy wire format retain their own existing version formats. This is a local-only compatibility change; no persisted Woven state was changed.

`node scripts/test-synthetic-archive-bounded-sealing.mjs` passed on the disposable local fixture. It covers bounded scan and short-page selector iteration; terminal-before-publication; a failed terminal CAS and lost acknowledgement with no published seal; interrupted seal publication; extra seal selectors before beginning and after publication; stale/foreign heads; corrupted encrypted checkpoint; legacy-mode rejection; both authority heads changing during staged `apply`; an authority change during staged `finish`; no downgrade; terminal-head CAS rejection and version-1 head rejection; marker rollback and removal during publication; and a legacy chunk writer, sealer and restore callback overtaken by marker creation. The fixture demonstrates reconciliation behavior using a mutable fake authority; resetting it is test-only, not an operational recovery procedure. `node scripts/test-synthetic-relocation-archive.mjs` also passed after the change.

The separate combined workerd importer test's later rerun has **no verified result**: its process was started with approved local loopback access but the subsequent poll was aborted before dispatch. Earlier combined workerd evidence predates these changes. No rerun or alternate observation was used to replace the interrupted action. These local tests do not cover representative data volume, a real Nextgen schema or Go handler, hosted R2, enforced authority/retention, real-project recovery, or route cutover. The representative archive-scale run and the real-owner and registry/history integrations remain separate open work.
