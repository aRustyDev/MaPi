# Archive restore integration requirements

Status: **production integration not implemented or verified**. Since this contract was written, an unmounted synthetic archive prototype and local staged restore tests have been added; see `synthetic-relocation-archive-local-proof.md` and `synthetic-archive-staged-local-proof.md` for their scope. They do not establish trusted commitment authority, independent storage or key custody, hosted retention, or real-owner recovery. This note remains an integration contract, not a cutover gate.

## Trusted inputs and access

- Obtain the authoritative frozen-source commitment from an independent control authority. It must bind the project and origin, source object and generation, move ID, schema and ownership manifest, ordered table set, complete chunk count, and final source digest. The recovery operator must authenticate this commitment independently of archive objects and the rewound source. A self-consistent archive alone is not proof that it is complete or belongs to the intended project.
- Provide a recovery-only credential for an independently durable archive store and an independently recoverable, access-controlled decryption key. Specify who may read, write, delete, replace, rotate and recover each, and validate those permissions in staging. A Worker binding, local mock, source-object copy, or bucket name alone is not evidence of independent custody or durability.
- Authenticate a final seal over the commitment and the complete ordered archive inventory. The seal must be distinguishable from an unfinished prefix, bind every archive object's authenticated metadata and exact ciphertext, and reject conflicting seals, duplicate indices, missing entries, extra entries and a mismatched project or move. The final format and key management must be shared with the writer; this note does not select them.

## Verification and staging

- Read bounded objects from the independent store and authenticate each before interpreting plaintext. Preserve the exact authenticated bytes; validate the format's version, object role, project, move, generation, index and manifest binding. Reject malformed envelopes, unsupported algorithms, wrong keys, truncated or extra bytes, altered ciphertext or authenticated metadata, nonce reuse, and unsupported versions. Do not emit keys, plaintext chunks, or object contents in logs or errors.
- Prove completeness against the trusted source commitment, not the archive's own claims. After all authenticated records have been read in their required order, validate the entire chunk chain and its final digest and count, the schema/ownership manifest, and the per-table counts and canonical digests. Do not accept a prefix, a reordered list, a conflicting duplicate, or an archive of another move. This requirement remains separate from the currently unverified staged-history integration.
- Stage the recovered records into a fresh, inaccessible owner under a separately checked recovery authority. Preserve exact types, identifiers, credentials, keys, sessions and audit history as required by the approved recovery policy. Check schema, generated values, indexes, sequences and foreign keys; compare the recovered data with the authenticated manifest before any activation. The archive verifier must never thaw the old source or activate a route by itself.
- Make reads and verification resumable from durable, authenticated checkpoints without skipping or reapplying committed work. A dropped acknowledgement is resolved through read-back; interrupted verification, missing objects, a failed authentication tag, a conflicting checkpoint or an unavailable key must leave recovery fenced. Concurrent or stale recovery attempts must not activate an unverified target.

## Evidence before use

Exercise the actual writer, independently provisioned storage and key recovery, and the actual restore path on a representative synthetic project. Test a correct complete archive; missing first, middle, final and seal objects; duplicated, reordered and cross-move objects; changed ciphertext, metadata and seal; wrong and unavailable keys; interrupted upload, verification and import; lost acknowledgements; worker or object eviction; and retries after restart. Verify exact project ID, users, credentials, audit history, per-table digests and foreign-key integrity, with a second project remaining isolated. Measure bounded object and memory use, throughput, checkpoint progress and total restore time.

Prove effective retention and access policy using the staging policy and independent credentials, including an attempted unauthorized deletion or overwrite where safe. Keep source replay chunks and the retired source fence until durable move completion, verified archive read-back and restore evidence, and the applicable retention/PITR policy permit cleanup. The real singleton still requires the separate staging gates, fresh inventory and backup verification, and explicit cutover authorization.
